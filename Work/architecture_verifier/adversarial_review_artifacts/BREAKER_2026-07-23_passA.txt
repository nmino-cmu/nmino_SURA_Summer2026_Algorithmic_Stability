# Independent Breaker Audit — Pass A

The Iter1 repair establishes strong object identity, but the operational security model is not yet defined. The active implementation block is necessary and correct; it is containment, not blueprint readiness.

## CRITICAL-01 — No authoritative mutation boundary exists

ART-07b requires all normative mutations to pass through an authoritative boundary, but explicitly defers that boundary and authenticated transitions to Iter3. The repair record likewise defers command/event APIs and says no authoritative write exists yet. ART-24 defines proposal creation but no corresponding commit interface, owner, validation contract, or atomic side-table update protocol.

Meanwhile, ART-06 already declares registries and committed events authoritative. Thus the package specifies authoritative state without specifying the exclusive mechanism that may create it.

Attack consequence: a store writer could mint or modify claims, proof attachments, definition heads, maturity records, audits, or human decisions without a defined authorization and invariant-enforcement point.

## CRITICAL-02 — Audit PASS can authorize an unbound or stale claim

The audit record binds `claim_ids[]` and registry IDs—not canonical `claim_digest` values, an immutable evidence closure, state/event root, SchemaRegistry version, audit-policy digest, or promotion-intent digest. Only definition-pin supersession is expressly said to invalidate PASS.

Nevertheless, PASS is ranked as authority in ART-05 and accepted as promotion evidence. This permits TOCTOU reuse after claim mappings, proof attachments, bridge state, evidence rows, counterexamples, or policy have changed. The record also tests `producer != auditor_id`, but `auditor_id` is absent from its schema.

Attack consequence: audit object A can be created against state S₀, then presented at S₁ for a changed or substituted claim/evidence closure. The package contains no normative freshness/revalidation predicate preventing authorization.

## HIGH-01 — Promotion lacks proof-carrying intent

ART-06’s promotion transaction consists of mutable identifiers and predicate booleans. It has no content-addressed intent, canonical claim digest, expected state/event root, evidence-closure digest, transition-policy version, authorization proof, nonce, or replay/idempotency binding. `audit_id` is optional.

ART-07b explicitly defers promotion intents to Iter5 and revokes ART-06’s `from_status/to_status` as normative promotion authority. ART-09 also declares its transition rules non-normative until Iter5.

Answer: under strict Iter1 authority, no promotion is valid at all. If the operational ART-06 path is implemented, promotion can indeed be attempted without proof-carrying intent.

## HIGH-02 — Authority is split-brain and unauthenticated

ART-05 places Human Decision at the top of the authority lattice and permits humans to propose writes, override audit verdicts, and authorize implementation. ART-06 treats `human_decisions` as authoritative registry rows.

ART-07b, however, defines only an opaque `actor_id` and makes HumanDecision non-authoritative until Iter4 authentication, roles, independence, and expiry enforcement exist. The two models are not reconciled by a package-wide fail-closed rule.

Attack consequence: a legacy interpretation accepts an unauthenticated decision row as top authority; the canonical interpretation rejects every such decision. This makes override, gate, and implementation-start authority non-deterministic.
