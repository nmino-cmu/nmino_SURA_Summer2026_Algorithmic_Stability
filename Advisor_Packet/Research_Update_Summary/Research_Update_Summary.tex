\section{Current research question}

\begin{tcolorbox}[notebox]
The current formulation studies uncertainty quantification when a hyperparameter
$\theta$ indexing an uncertainty set $\mathcal{C}(X;\mathcal{D},\theta)$ is chosen by
constrained optimization on the \emph{same} calibration data $\mathcal{D}$ used to
construct the set.
Fixed-hyperparameter coverage need not survive this data-dependent choice.
The project asks (i)~when validity remains after data-dependent selection,
(ii)~how far actual miscoverage can deviate from the nominal level, and
(iii)~whether coverage can be restored by randomizing the
\emph{selection} map (stabilizing randomness $\xi$) while retaining the existing
fixed-$\theta$ construction of $\mathcal{C}$.
\end{tcolorbox}

\noindent\textbf{Precision.}
The map $\mathcal{C}(\,\cdot\,;\mathcal{D},\theta)$ for fixed, prespecified $\theta$ is unchanged.
What changes is the procedure that produces $\theta$ (deterministic
$\hat\theta(\mathcal{D})$ vs.\ randomized $\widetilde\theta(\mathcal{D};\xi)$).
Changing the selected hyperparameter generally changes the realized set.
