\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\section{Major intellectual contributions}

\subsection{Formulation I --- broad stability framework (20 hours)}
\begin{tcolorbox}[featurebox, breakable=false, title=Formulation I]
Earlier June work (June~25 email; June~29 meeting) developed a broad framework connecting
algorithmic stability for post-hoc selection; optimization-based selectors; primitive
decompositions; stabilization via noise, randomization, or regularization; reduced data
splitting; and downstream inference.

\textbf{What was learned.} Concrete algorithms share recurring patterns once decomposed into
primitives---motivating a catalog-driven search rather than a one-off construction.
The June~25 framework was \emph{student-proposed} and not yet approved.
\end{tcolorbox}

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{tcolorbox}[catalogbox]
{\large\bfseries\color{navy} Stabilized Algorithm Catalog (20 hours)}\\[3pt]
A primary research contribution: structured extraction of algorithms that had already been
stabilized in the literature, used to motivate Formulation~I and later support Formulation~II.
\begin{itemize}
  \item Identified concrete selection procedures that had been stabilized, and extracted the
        original algorithm together with the selected object.
  \item Recorded the stability notion, assumptions, and the stabilization mechanism
        (noise, randomization, or regularization), including noise distributions where relevant.
  \item Decomposed each algorithm into primitive operations and compared utility costs.
  \item Recorded proof strategies and downstream inference or generalization guarantees.
  \item Searched for recurring structural patterns across examples.
  \item Converted the results into a structured database and LaTeX artifacts that informed
        the later optimization-selected UQ formulation.
\end{itemize}
\end{tcolorbox}

\subsection{Formulation II --- optimization-selected uncertainty quantification (10 hours)}
\begin{tcolorbox}[featurebox, breakable=false, title=Formulation II]
Narrower current problem: uncertainty sets from calibration data whose hyperparameter is
selected by constrained optimization on the \emph{same} data.
\begin{itemize}
  \item Validity after data-dependent hyperparameter selection; miscoverage deviation.
  \item Part~I: replace-one stability of $\hat\theta(\mathcal{D})$.
  \item Part~II: diagnose post-selection coverage; restore coverage by randomizing selection.
  \item Retain the fixed-$\theta$ uncertainty set construction while changing
        \emph{how} the hyperparameter is selected (realized set generally changes with $\theta$).
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Decomposition.} Part~I: replace-one stability of $\hat\theta(\mathcal{D})$.
Part~II: validity and miscoverage after data-dependent selection; restore coverage by
randomizing selection (no recalibration of $\mathcal{C}$).
\end{tcolorbox}

\subsection{Supporting infrastructure}
Discovery architecture and a modest formalization library support the mathematical program.
They should enable---not replace---the Part~I theorem.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\section{Current formulation}

\begin{tcolorbox}[basebox]
\textbf{Research question.}
Study when an uncertainty set $\mathcal{C}(X;\mathcal{D},\theta)$ remains valid after its
hyperparameter is selected by constrained optimization on the same calibration data
$\mathcal{D}$; quantify validity or miscoverage deviation; restore coverage by randomizing
the selection map while retaining the existing fixed-$\theta$ uncertainty set construction.

The map $\mathcal{C}(\,\cdot\,;\mathcal{D},\theta)$ for fixed, prespecified $\theta$ is
unchanged. Changing the selected hyperparameter
($\hat\theta(\mathcal{D})$ or $\widetilde\theta(\mathcal{D};\xi)$) generally changes the
realized set.
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{Part I.} Replace-one stability of $\hat\theta(\mathcal{D})$ under explicit assumptions.

\textbf{Part II.} Validity and miscoverage after data-dependent selection; restore coverage by
randomizing selection (no recalibration of $\mathcal{C}$). Part~I informs diagnosis;
goal~(iii) modifies the selection map.

\textbf{Immediate theorem target.} Prove Part~I for the motivating problem; obtain advisor
feedback; only then extend.
