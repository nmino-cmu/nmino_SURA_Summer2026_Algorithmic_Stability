```latex
\begin{theorem}[Set-valued stability transfer]
\label{thm:set-valued-stability-transfer}
Let
\[
W=(D,X,Y)
\]
denote the full inference experiment, with law \(P\), and let
\(\Theta\) be the measurable space of tuning parameters.  For every
\(q\in(0,1)\) and \(\theta\in\Theta\), let
\[
C^{(q)}(X;D,\theta)\subseteq\mathcal Y
\]
be a measurable, possibly randomized uncertainty set.  Define its failure
indicator by
\[
\ell_q(W,\theta)
    :=\mathbf 1\!\left\{Y\notin C^{(q)}(X;D,\theta)\right\}.
\]

Let \(\widehat\theta=A(D)\), where \(A\) may use auxiliary randomization.
Suppose that \(A\) is \((\eta,\tau,\nu)\)-stable in the sense that there
exist

\begin{enumerate}
\item a random oracle output \(\theta_0=A_0\), independent of \(W\), and
\item an event \(G\in\sigma(W)\), with \(P(G)\geq 1-\nu\),
\end{enumerate}

such that, for every \(w\in G\), the conditional law \(K_w\) of
\(A(D)\) given \(W=w\) and the law \(Q_0\) of \(A_0\) satisfy
\[
K_w(B)\leq e^\eta Q_0(B)+\tau
\qquad
\text{for every measurable }B\subseteq\Theta.
\tag{ST}
\]
The randomization used to construct \(C^{(q)}\), if any, is included in
\(W\), or is conditionally generated by the same Markov kernel after
either \(A(D)\) or \(A_0\) is supplied.

Assume the oracle-validity condition
\[
\mathbb P\!\left\{
Y\notin C^{(q)}(X;D,A_0)
\right\}\leq q.
\tag{OV}
\]
Then
\[
\mathbb P\!\left\{
Y\notin C^{(q)}(X;D,\widehat\theta)
\right\}
\leq e^\eta q+\tau+\nu.
\tag{2}
\]
Consequently, taking \(q=\delta e^{-\eta}\) gives
\[
\mathbb P\!\left\{
Y\notin
C^{(\delta e^{-\eta})}(X;D,\widehat\theta)
\right\}
\leq \delta+\tau+\nu.
\tag{3}
\]
\end{theorem}
```

Here \(C^{(q)}\) denotes the set calibrated at miscoverage level \(q\). This is the direct set-valued analogue of the Zrnic–Jordan transfer theorem ([published paper](https://doi.org/10.1214/23-AOS2303)).

### Proof sketch

```latex
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Fix \(q\in(0,1)\).  For \(w=(d,x,y)\), define the measurable failure
section
\[
B_q(w)
  :=\left\{
       \theta\in\Theta:
       y\notin C^{(q)}(x;d,\theta)
     \right\}.
\]
Conditional on \(W=w\),
\[
\mathbb P\!\left\{
Y\notin C^{(q)}(X;D,\widehat\theta)
\,\middle|\, W=w
\right\}
=K_w(B_q(w)).
\]
For \(w\in G\), stability \emph{may be applied to this
\(w\)-dependent section}, because \((\mathrm{ST})\) holds for every
measurable subset of \(\Theta\):
\[
K_w(B_q(w))
 \leq e^\eta Q_0(B_q(w))+\tau.
\]
On \(G^c\), the conditional failure probability is at most one.
Therefore,
\begin{align*}
\mathbb P\!\left\{
Y\notin C^{(q)}(X;D,\widehat\theta)
\right\}
&\leq
e^\eta\,
\mathbb E\!\left[Q_0(B_q(W))\right]
+\tau+\mathbb P(G^c)                                                     \\
&\leq
e^\eta\,
\mathbb P\!\left\{
Y\notin C^{(q)}(X;D,A_0)
\right\}
+\tau+\nu                                                               \\
&\leq e^\eta q+\tau+\nu.
\end{align*}
The second line uses the independence of \(A_0\) and \(W\), together
with the requirement that any downstream randomization in the
uncertainty-set construction is coupled through the same Markov kernel.
Substituting \(q=\delta e^{-\eta}\) proves \((3)\).
\end{proof}
```

## Case (A): joint fixed-\(\theta\) validity

A simple sufficient condition for `(OV)` is

```latex
\[
\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}
\mathbb P\!\left\{
Y\notin C^{(q)}(X;D,\theta)
\right\}
\leq q,
\tag{FV}
\]
```

where the probability is over the full joint law of \((D,X,Y)\) and all uncertainty-set randomization, while \(\theta\) is held fixed.

Indeed, since \(A_0\perp (D,X,Y)\),

```latex
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P\!\left\{
Y\notin C^{(q)}(X;D,A_0)
\right\}
&=
\int_\Theta
\mathbb P\!\left\{
Y\notin C^{(q)}(X;D,\theta)
\right\}
\,Q_0(d\theta) \\
&\leq q.
\end{aligned}
\]
```

Thus dependence of \(C\) on \(D\) is not itself an obstruction. What matters is that fixed-\(\theta\) validity is genuinely joint over the same \(D\) and test variables appearing after selection. A statement conditional on a nonrandom training set, or a statement proved for an independent copy of \(D\), does not automatically imply `(FV)`.

## Case (B): selection-coupled set construction

If the construction of \(C\) uses randomness or data-dependent choices coupled with the execution of \(A\), marginal fixed-\(\theta\) validity alone is insufficient. One needs either:

```latex
\[
\mathbb P\!\left\{
Y\notin C^{(q)}(X;D,A_0)
\right\}\leq q,
\tag{OV$'$}
\]
```

for the actual oracle-coupled construction, together with stability of the full coupled mechanism; or, more structurally:

```latex
\[
(D,X,Y,U_C)\perp A_0,
\]
```

where \(U_C\) contains all randomness and intermediate choices used to construct \(C\), and the conditional law of \(U_C\) given \((D,X,Y,\theta)\) is the same whether \(\theta=A(D)\) or \(\theta=A_0\).

Equivalently, the failure indicator must be a common measurable post-processing
\[
\ell_q(W,U_C,\theta)
\]
of the stable output \(\theta\). If replacing \(A(D)\) by \(A_0\) also changes the law of the data or set-construction randomness, stability of \(A(D)\) alone does not transfer validity; one must instead establish \((\eta,\tau,\nu)\)-indistinguishability for the enlarged output containing every selection-coupled quantity used by \(C\).

## Goal-(iii) corollary (W5: fixed classical level, no recalibration)

```latex
\begin{corollary}[Vanishing inflation at fixed classical level]
\label{cor:vanishing-stability-correction}
Suppose that a family of randomized selection rules
$\{A_r\}_{r\geq1}$ is
$(\eta_r,\tau_r,\nu_r)$-stable, with
\[
\eta_r\to0,
\qquad
\tau_r+\nu_r\to0,
\]
and that randomizing $A_r$ does not alter the fixed-$\theta$ map
\[
(D,X)\longmapsto C^{(\alpha)}(X;D,\theta)
\]
(same classical level $\alpha$ as writeup (1)).
If $(\mathrm{FV})$ holds for that unchanged map, then by the set-valued
transfer theorem with $q=\alpha$,
\[
\mathbb P\!\left\{
Y\notin
C^{(\alpha)}(X;D,A_r(D))
\right\}
\leq e^{\eta_r}\alpha+\tau_r+\nu_r,
\]
hence the inflation
$\mathrm{Infl}_r\le(e^{\eta_r}-1)\alpha+\tau_r+\nu_r\to0$.
If $\eta=\tau=\nu=0$, then
\[
\mathbb P\!\left\{
Y\in C^{(\alpha)}(X;D,A_r(D))
\right\}\geq 1-\alpha.
\]
\end{corollary}
```

at the **same** family $C^{(\alpha)}$ without reconstructing $C$.

Minimal assumptions:

1. Joint measurability of $(w,\theta)\mapsto\mathbf 1\{y\notin C^{(q)}(x;d,\theta)\}$.
2. Fixed-$\theta$ validity over the actual joint inference experiment, or directly oracle validity `(OV)`.
3. An oracle output independent of the full inference experiment, not merely marginally independent of $D$.
4. Pointwise-on-a-good-event max-divergence control `(ST)`, sufficient for the data-dependent sections $B_q(W)`.
5. Common downstream post-processing: no unaccounted shared randomness or intermediate selection output coupling $A$ to $C$.
6. For goal (iii): $\eta\to0$ and $\tau+\nu\to0$; randomization changes only selection, not the fixed-$\theta$ uncertainty-set map **nor** the classical level $\alpha$.
